C2PA Conformance Program and Explorer

Media and imagesregistryactive
The C2PA Conformance Program is the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity's risk-based governance program that designates generator and validator products as conforming and holds certification authorities to the C2PA Certificate Policy. Administered by the C2PA Conformance Task Force, it opened in June 2025 and is now at version 0.2; its unit is the product on the public Conforming Products List.

The C2PA Conformance Program provides assurance that products adhere to the Content Credentials specification, and fulfill a set of security requirements to ensure they are producing and validating C2PA data correctly.

In its own words: https://c2pa.org/conformance/, read 2026-10-29

What it is

Its stated purpose is assurance "that products adhere to the Content Credentials specification, and fulfill a set of security requirements to ensure they are producing and validating C2PA data correctly." The program document says the specification "is limited without a robust governance framework and conformance program." It belongs to a C2PA Governance Framework governed by the Steering Committee; the Conformance Task Force, established in 2024, is co-chaired by Andy Parsons, Yogesh Deshpande and Sherif Hanna. The Content Authenticity Initiative likens the program to the Bluetooth and Wi-Fi programs.

From its /non article, sentences 1, 2, 3

How it works

Applicants sign a legal agreement with the C2PA and provide evidence; the technical evaluation checks that manifests are properly formatted and certificates handled per the Certificate Policy. An accepted product goes on the Conforming Products List, and the generator product, always the signer, is accountable for the assets it generates. Only conforming generator products can receive claim-signing certificates from a Certification Authority on the C2PA Trust List.

From its /non article, sentences 4, 6, 12

Two assurance levels are in operation. Level 2 demands hardware-backed key storage and dynamic security evidence, and Google's Pixel 10 was the first device at the highest level. When the census read the JSON list it held 286 records, 254 generator and 32 validator products, 11 generators at maximum level 2.

From its /non article, sentences 11, 15

What it leaves out

C2PA states the limit itself: Content Credentials "do not provide value judgments about whether a given set of provenance data is 'true'," and "provenance information alone cannot tell you whether the digital content is true, accurate or factual." The core specification "does not support attribution of content to individuals or organizations." Provenance is not always complete: the metadata can be removed and manifests separated, and an ingredient's provenance cannot be verified in the same way as the containing asset's.

From its /non article, sentences 18, 20, 22

On security the sources conflict: the FAQ says faking credentials would require breaking current cryptographic standards, while Wikipedia reports a vulnerability demonstrated in October 2026 that let an image be altered without invalidating its signatures. Adoption is partial: as of 2026 no dedicated camera had achieved compliance, and IPTC Metawatch found 27 C2PA manifests in 6,580 news images in October 2026.

From its /non article, sentences 24, 25

Place in the field

In the archive's reading the program certifies the artifact dimension of provenance, which "verifies that *this file* was created by *this source* at *this time*." Past the artifact the archive finds no reach: "When a 5,000-word article becomes a 200-word AI Overview, C2PA cannot tell you what was lost."

From its /non article, sentences 17, 27

At the name, Google's AI Overview composed the program as its applicants meet it, components and steps, and offered end-to-end tamper evidence for digital media while omitting C2PA's own negative tags. In the Capture Registry's reading, "A reader leaves with tamper evidence and without the caveat that well-formed provenance can be false."
