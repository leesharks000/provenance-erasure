Getty Provenance Index

Objects and artdatabase (linked open data)active
The Getty Provenance Index is the Getty Research Institute's database of art provenance: over 12 million digitized, open-access resources, drawn from archival records of the 16th to the 20th centuries, that document historic transfers of art ownership. Its unit is a resource drawn from a primary document, and the Index is moving to linked open data on the Arches platform.

The Getty Provenance Index® (GPI) contains over 12 million digitized, open-access resources that trace the origins, movements, and lineages of art and cultural artifacts.

In its own words: https://www.getty.edu/databases-tools-and-technologies/provenance/, read 2026-10-29

What it is

One name holds two things: "an active research initiative carried out by Getty staff that supports an evolving data resource with the same name." The records are used to trace "the ownership, movement, and histories of artworks," with strengths in European and American art.

From its /non article, sentences 2, 3, 7

What it holds

It draws on transcribed primary documents of three kinds. The dealer stock books are those of two firms: the 15 stock books of Goupil & Cie./Boussod, Valadon & Cie in Paris (1846–1919) and the 11 painting stock books of M. Knoedler & Co. in New York (1872–1970), each over 190,000 resources. The sales catalogs document works offered for sale in Europe from 1650 to 1945, over 12 million resources from more than 22,000 catalogs. The archival inventories, legal records of European private collections from 1520 to 1880, hold over 276,000 records from more than 13,000 documents.

From its /non article, sentences 9, 10, 11

Three further databases, the Collectors Files, Payments to Artists and Public Collections, complement the Index on its home page and form its foundation on its coverage page. They hold about 20,000 collectors' folders, about 1,000 payments recorded in Rome between 1576 and 1711, and over 95,000 public-collection records from 1500 to 1990.

From its /non article, sentences 12, 13

History

The Getty's own pages state the founding three ways: founded in 1974 by Burton Fredericksen in the Getty Museum's Paintings department; begun in the early 1980s as an initiative led by curator Burton B. Fredericksen; and "1981 – present."

From its /non article, sentence 14

First compiled digitally in 1984, published on CD-ROM in 1994 and launched online in 1998, it had become a dedicated department by 2004 and is headed now by Sandra van Ginhoven. The Remodeled Index on Arches holds the dealer stock books and sales catalogs; archival inventory data are to follow, and the original datasets remain in the Legacy Index.

From its /non article, sentences 16, 17, 18

Access and limits

Machine access runs through API documentation with guidance on licences and attribution, and through CSV exports of the Legacy Index on GitHub. Its stated limit is coverage: "The quantity and scope of research material available in the Getty Provenance Index® varies by region, period, and type of document."

From its /non article, sentences 20, 21

Place in the field

The archive counts art-historical provenance, the "Documented chain of ownership and attribution for art objects," as an adjacent tradition to semantic provenance, with the "Same conceptual structure; different object." At the name, Google's AI Overview composed the index with its scale and maintainer right and its origin left out: of the two founding statements on the Getty's pages it gives neither and names no person, and it joins the two firms' stock books under one count and one span, at a provenance erasure rate of 0.5.
