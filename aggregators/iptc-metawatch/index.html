IPTC Metawatch

Media and imagesmonitoractive
IPTC Metawatch is the International Press Telecommunications Council's free, open monitor of whether news publishers keep the credits, captions and rights information embedded in their photographs, announced on 5 October 2026. On the first of each month its crawler reads the lead photographs of up to 20 recent articles from each of 508 news publishers in 122 countries.

How it works

The crawler checks Exif, IPTC Photo Metadata and C2PA Content Credentials in every lead photograph and scores four fields, creator, caption, copyright and credit line, the "Four Cs" of news photo provenance; other fields are tracked for context. It is "deliberately light": about 40 to 60 requests per publisher a month, honouring robots.txt and keeping no article text or image files. Every run is published as open data under CC BY 4.0, and publishers may opt out by email.

From its /non article, sentences 4, 5

What it tests

What it tests is a transformation, publication through a news site's pipeline: credits "are lost between camera and reader" as content management systems re-encode images, delivery networks resize them and optimisation tools discard what looks like unnecessary bytes. It builds on IMATAG's 2018 State of Image Metadata study and the IPTC's own prototype crawler of 2019, and differs from them in measuring every month; the IPTC's 2019 social-media test is a separate series.

From its /non article, sentences 6, 8

What it found

In the October run 7.2% of editorial images carried a credit or copyright notice, against IMATAG's 8% in 2018, and 321 of the 425 publishers it could crawl kept none of the scored IPTC fields. Images through Cloudflare, Akamai, CloudFront and Fastly lost their metadata 87% to 95% of the time, and images with no delivery network 87%, so that, in the announcement, "Stripping appears to happen in the publishing pipeline"; the per-field page reads the same data as publishers and CDNs discarding metadata embedded upstream.

From its /non article, sentences 13, 15

Some publishers moved: Les Echos rose from 6% to 75%, and USA Today reached 95% of lead photographs carrying credit, creator and copyright in September. Of 505 publishers checked, 234 block at least one AI crawler in robots.txt, while the PLUS Data Mining field appears on 0.6% of images. 27 of 6,580 images carried a C2PA manifest, all but one signed by an AI or design tool.

From its /non article, sentences 18, 19, 20

Place in the field

The monitor states its limits: the United States slipped from first to fourth in October when AP and USA Today began blocking automated access, "a reminder that bot-blocking aimed at AI also shuts out research," and publishers reached without a photograph are listed as no_images. The archive holds the artifact dimension of provenance necessary and insufficient: C2PA "prevents forgery; it does not constitute authorship."

From its /non article, sentences 24, 25

At Google's AI Overview the Capture Registry finds the method and scale composed and the findings absent: "The composition gives what the monitor checks and none of what it found," the IPTC photo metadata capture's pattern "in small: the carrier composed, the loss omitted."
