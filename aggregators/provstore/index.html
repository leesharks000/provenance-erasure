ProvStore

Computational lineagerepository (retired)ended
ProvStore was a free public repository for W3C PROV provenance documents, storing, browsing and managing them through a web interface or a REST API. Led by Dong Huynh, it ran at the University of Southampton from 2013 and at King's College London from 2018, and was retired in December 2024.

A provenance repository that allows storing, browsing, and managing provenance documents via a Web interface or a REST API.

In its own words: https://openprovenance.org/, read 2026-10-29

What it was

Its paper calls it "the first online public provenance repository supporting the new PROV standards by W3C." Users and applications could store and optionally publish provenance, and documents "could be transformed, visualised and shared in various file formats, with all the functionality also available via a RESTful API."

From its /non article, sentences 2, 3

Its unit was a provenance document, carried in PROV-N, PROV-JSON, PROV-XML and other serializations, and the relations it held were those of the PROV model: entities, activities, agents and derivations.

From its /non article, sentence 4

The standard

W3C PROV is, in its maintainer's words, "the current standard for provenance (by the World Wide Web Consortium)." Huynh took part in the Provenance Working Group at W3C and began the PROV Python package during it, in 2012. Before PROV stood the Open Provenance Model, which the operator's page files under "Provenance Legacy." The OpenProvenance suite offers beside ProvStore a validator, a translator between PROV representations, and PROV libraries in Java, Python and JavaScript.

From its /non article, sentences 6, 7, 16

How it ended

Huynh's post of 8 December 2016 carries the note "Updated 12-Sep-2026 : ProvStore was retired in December 2024. Its entries below are kept as plain text." The OpenProvenance landing page still says "ProvStore is coming back soon." The census keeps both, under the tag "a live landing page is not a live repository"; whether the public documents were archived anywhere is unchecked.

From its /non article, sentences 17, 18, 19, 20

Place in the field

The archive reads W3C PROV as the closest technical cousin of semantic provenance: it "Operates on data flow; semantic provenance operates on meaning circulation." PROV-O, on the same reading, can record that a metadata packet was created by a DID, while the archive's packets record whether its claims are consistent with the entity's position in a citation environment.
