Schoenberg Database of Manuscripts

Manuscripts and booksdatabaseactive
The Schoenberg Database of Manuscripts (SDBM) is an online, user-maintained, open-access database run by the Schoenberg Institute for Manuscript Studies at the University of Pennsylvania, aggregating historic observations of pre-modern manuscripts drawn from over 14,000 auction and sales catalogs, inventories, collection catalogs and other sources. Its unit is the observation: an entry in a source that documents a manuscript.

The SDBM continuously aggregates and updates historic observations of pre-modern manuscripts drawn from over 14,000 auction and sales catalogs, inventories, catalogs from institutional and private collections, and other sources that document the sales and locations of these books from around the world.

In its own words: https://sdbm.library.upenn.edu/, read 2026-10-29

What it is

It documents the sales and locations of manuscripts from around the world. At the core of its Institute is the Lawrence J. Schoenberg collection of manuscripts, donated to the Penn Libraries in 2011; the collection and the database are distinct.

From its /non article, sentences 2, 4, 5

It is built by its users: members are invited to contribute their "own personal observations of a manuscript or group of manuscripts" singly or by batch upload. It may be searched or browsed by author, title, seller, provenance and date, downloaded in .xlsx or .csv format, and queried through a public SPARQL endpoint.

From its /non article, sentences 19, 20

How it works

Each record is an observation of a manuscript at a point in time, in a sales or auction catalogue or a collection catalogue, and two or more observations of one manuscript are linked by a manuscript record. Its relations are sale, location and ownership.

From its /non article, sentences 6, 8

Its carriers include a VIAF-based name authority, enhanced provenance data and a SPARQL endpoint; its provenance facet as read is headed by the Bibliothèque nationale de France (23,741), Sir Thomas Phillipps (21,582) and the British Library (7,822). The Institute is ineligible to participate in VIAF, and through Mapping Manuscript Migrations hundreds of names were corrected for authority control.

From its /non article, sentences 9, 10

Its counts

The counts are of sources and of records, each with its date: over 13,000 sources in 2015, over 14,000 in 2020 and on the home page as read, and more than 240,000 records of manuscript observations in May 2020. The home page's source-type facet gave 122,751 collection catalogues, 99,319 auction and dealer catalogues and 19,859 unpublished sources in 2020, and 129,525, 106,401 and 23,861 in 2026.

From its /non article, sentences 16, 17

What its selection leaves out

It tracks generic titles, and works in the specific sense FRBR employs are outside it; a manuscript's current location is not specifically present, though it can often be inferred from the most recent observation; and its entries for sales and gifts carried no place information. Six of the research questions Mapping Manuscript Migrations set could not be answered from it alone. Downloaded search results are offered under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence.

From its /non article, sentences 24, 21

Place in the field

It is one of the three source datasets of Mapping Manuscript Migrations, the 2017–2020 project that turned them into Linked Open Data without correcting them. At its address the Capture Registry judges Google's AI Overview "The best-composed of the census rows," with host, unit, scale and structure at the database's own grain and only the licence, the condition of reuse, left out; the Institute and Penn Libraries are named, and no person or identifier, at a provenance erasure rate of 0.5.
