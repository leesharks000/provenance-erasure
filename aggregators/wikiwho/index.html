WikiWho

Authorship and reusealgorithm and APIactive
WikiWho is an algorithm and API that attributes authorship of Wikipedia articles at the level of the token, giving for every word the revision and editor that introduced it and every removal and reintroduction since. First developed at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology and GESIS, it has been maintained since August 2021 by Wikimedia Community Tech and the Wiki Education Foundation.

The WikiWho API gives access to the provenance/changes of words (tokens) in Wikipedia articles.

In its own words: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gesiscss/wikiwho_api_public/master/README.md, read 2026-10-29

What it is

The algorithm, published by Fabian Flöck and Maribel Acosta at WWW 2014, "parses the complete set of all historical revisions (versions) in order to find out who wrote and/or removed and/or reinserted which exact text at token level at what revision." Tokens retain a unique ID, so that identical strings in different positions stay distinct, and for each one "its individual addition, removal, and reintroduction history becomes available." Its makers report "extensive evaluation resulting in 95% accuracy on fairly revision-rich articles."

What is built on it

Further APIs deliver HTML markup for interfaces: the WhoColor API delivers annotated HTML of a wiki article, colouring the text by its original authors. It powers the Who Wrote That? browser extension and features of the Wiki Education Dashboard and the Programs and Events Dashboard, and XTools Authorship, XTools Blame and pywikibot's authorship method are also built on WikiWho's data and API.

From its /non article, sentences 9, 10

Its makers name conflict research among its uses: an extension reads "(re)delete" and "reintroduce" actions between editors as antagonistic and supporting, and the data serve for extracting disputes about content. TokTrack (2017) carries its data as a complete token provenance and change tracking dataset for the English Wikipedia.

From its /non article, sentences 12, 13

History

It was first conceived as a dissertation project of Fabian Flöck at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology and was hosted and developed at GESIS – Leibniz Institute for the Social Sciences. In August 2021 it moved to Wikimedia Cloud Services; Wikipedia's account has Flöck planning in 2021 to leave GESIS and shut it down in 2022 while a Wikimedia engineer migrated the infrastructure.

From its /non article, sentences 14, 15, 16

Its address is wikiwho.wmcloud.org, which lists 70 WikiWho and 70 WhoColor language endpoints. A Meta research page, last edited in 2024, offers four languages, English, German, Turkish and Basque; the MediaWiki page, last edited on 15 July 2026, lists 71 supported wikis, 70 distinct, with Swedish Wikipedia twice.
