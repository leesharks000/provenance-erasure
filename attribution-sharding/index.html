The method. A capture is a dated, preservable record of one machine composition against a source-known tracer corpus: screenshot, verbatim transcript, four-state provenance coding over four atomic tokens, and per-output token preservation. A fixed twelve-query battery was captured in full on 9–10 June 2026 and recaptured, queries verbatim, on 30 July 2026: 24 documented events, one June null kept as a null.

Two measures, kept apart. Attribution presence per query fell from 10/11 scorable outputs to 6/12. Conditional provenance erasure, the unattributed reproductions among outputs that reproduce framework-specific content, rose from 1/11 to 2/8.

Across a severance. Between the two captures the platform hosting the corpus's certifying identifiers terminated the depositing account. Queries whose June attribution resolved through since-deleted records lost attribution at 3/6, against 1/4 in the unaffected stratum: a severance-associated attribution-loss differential of +0.25, consistent with repository removal as the operative mechanism; the paired observational design cannot exclude concurrent system changes.

Where the loss lies. Concept-grade content persists through residual metadata, while specification-grade, computable content ceased to render in the observed outputs. The deepest cost of repository deletion is in the operative stratum: retrievable, computable instruments.
