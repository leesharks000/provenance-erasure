 About the Getty Provenance Index 

 The Getty Provenance Index® (GPI) contains over 12 million digitized, open-access resources that trace the origins, movements, and lineages of art and cultural artifacts. Spanning the 16th–20th centuries, the GPI includes primary source materials such as transcribed inventories, auction catalogs, and dealer stock books, with strengths in European and American art.

 Founded in 1974 by Burton Fredericksen, the GPI was created to improve access to information found in archival documents related to the ownership of art. Originating in the Getty Museum’s Paintings department, the GPI is the product of continued international collaboration and has expanded to include information from the Getty Research Institute's own archives related to dealers and the art trade.

 As one of the earliest digital art history initiatives, the GPI has continually operated at the forefront of technological advancement and open access. First compiled digitally in 1984, published on CD-ROM in 1994, and launched online in 1998, it has evolved alongside advances in provenance research and digital art history. More recently, the GPI has transitioned to a Linked Open Data (LOD) framework, enabling network visualization and cross-database analysis.

 Today, the GPI continues to grow as a leading nexus of provenance research, forging new partnerships and integrating technological advances.

 Read answers to frequently asked questions about the Getty Provenance Index

 What is the Getty Provenance Index and how is it used? 
 The Getty Provenance Index® provides open access to millions of richly curated records that document historic transfers of art ownership. It is used to trace the ownership, movement, and histories of artworks, providing essential data for provenance research and the study of cultural heritage.

 What types of records are included in the Getty Provenance Index? 
 The Getty Provenance Index includes records from Dealer Stock Books, Sales Catalogs, and Archival Inventories. The Getty Research Institute also provides separate access to provenance resources like Payments to Artists, Collectors Files, and Public Collections. For more information about these datasets, see What's Covered .

 How do I access the Getty Provenance Index? 
 Information from Dealer Stock Books and Sales Catalogs are accessible in a linked open data structure through a Getty-supported, open-source database platform called Arches . Access to Archival Inventories records are currently available on our legacy database platform , but will soon be remodeled as linked open data and migrated to Arches. The Payments to Artists, Collectors Files, and Public Collections datasets are available through their current platform .
